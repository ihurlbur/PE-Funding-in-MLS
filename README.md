# PE-Funding-in-MLS
Strategic analysis of how private equity investment is reshaping Major League Soccer’s revenue model, sponsorship strategy, and fan experience. The project evaluates both the commercial benefits of institutional capital and the long-term risks created by investor-driven growth. This was a group project as part of USC's "MOR 579 - The Business of Sports Entertainment" class.

## Overview
This project examines how private equity has accelerated MLS’s transition from a locally focused, matchday-dependent business model toward a more centralized, scalable, and globally oriented commercial system.

The analysis focuses on:
- How private equity funds new commercial capabilities and infrastructure
- Why investor incentives encourage recurring and scalable revenue streams
- How centralization supports league-wide media and sponsorship opportunities
- The impact of premiumization and digital-first operations on fan accessibility
- The tension between short-term investor returns and long-term league sustainability

## Objectives
- Compare MLS’s commercial model before and after increased private equity involvement
- Explain how private equity capital and valuation priorities influence league strategy
- Evaluate changes to media rights, sponsorships, stadium experiences, and digital engagement
- Analyze San Diego FC and Inter Miami as examples of PE-backed commercial development
- Compare MLS’s approach with private equity investment in other sports leagues
- Recommend policies that balance commercial growth with accessibility and local identity

## Project Context
- This presentation was developed as a group project by Nick Arnold, Ryan Cornish, Ian Hurlburt, and Michael Medina.
- The project applies a private equity framework to evaluate how outside capital can influence the commercial strategy and cultural identity of a professional sports league.
- San Diego FC and Inter Miami are used as case studies to illustrate how institutional investment can affect sponsorship development, stadium experiences, and fan access.
- The analysis is intended for academic purposes and does not represent the views of Major League Soccer, its clubs, investors, or commercial partners.
